Home / Research

Research for more dependable AI.

I study how language models create knowledge, where factual or contextual failures emerge, and how evaluation and workflow design can make generation more reliable.

01 · Hallucination

Hallucination detection and quantification

My work investigates unsupported and contextually inconsistent generations, particularly in scientific writing. I am interested in both measurement—how hallucination is detected and quantified—and in the behaviors that make these errors more likely.

Contextual consistencyFaithfulnessLLM-as-judge
02 · Scientific NLP

Reliable scientific knowledge generation

I build and evaluate resources for scientific text, including benchmarks and datasets for fine-grained hallucination detection. A central goal is to make generated scholarly content easier to evaluate and trust.

Scientific writingBenchmarkingDataset design
03 · Agentic AI

Reliability of decomposed and multi-agent workflows

I examine whether breaking a knowledge-generation task across specialized agents improves quality consistently, what coordination costs are introduced, and how errors can be localized and repaired.

Task decompositionProvenanceTargeted repair
04 · Evaluation

AI evaluation for reliability and trustworthiness

Across projects, I am interested in evaluation frameworks that connect task success, grounding, factual accuracy, writing quality, and computational cost rather than relying on a single aggregate score.

Controlled evaluationHuman validationRobustness

From detection to intervention.

My research trajectory increasingly focuses on not only finding failures, but understanding which failures can be repaired, where they originate, and what repair costs.
